Project Details
Martian Guide is an integrated digital exploration and astronaut-support platform consisting of two interconnected modules: 3D Mars Explorer and Astronaut Helmet HUD. Together, they demonstrate how planetary visualization, mission information, and astronaut monitoring interfaces can contribute to safer and more informed human exploration of Mars.
1. 3D Mars Explorer
The 3D Mars Explorer provides an interactive three-dimensional representation of Mars, allowing users to explore the planet and examine important elements of its exploration history and geography.
Its key features include:
Martian Orbital Systems: Visualization of Mars's artificial satellites (orbiters), illustrating humanity's orbital exploration of the planet.
Natural Satellites: Representation of both Martian moons, Phobos and Deimos.
Rover Exploration Routes: Visualization of the exploration journeys of NASA's Curiosity and Perseverance rovers, connecting their missions to the Martian landscape.
Geographical Landmarks: Exploration of prominent Martian locations and geological formations, including Olympus Mons and other notable regions.
Geological Structure Visualization: Interactive controls allow users to show or hide different geological layers of Mars, represented through distinct colors (red, yellow, and blue), providing a visual understanding of the planet's internal structure.
These features transform Mars from a distant astronomical object into an interactive environment that users can investigate and understand.
2. Astronaut Helmet Heads-Up Display (HUD)
The second component is an astronaut-oriented Heads-Up Display designed to simulate the essential information an astronaut might need during an extravehicular activity (EVA) on Mars.
The interface presents simulated planetary and spacesuit telemetry, including:
Oxygen levels
Spacesuit pressure
CO₂ scrubber status
Internal and environmental temperature
Radiation levels
Astronaut heart rate
Navigation coordinates
Mission elapsed time
By organizing these parameters in a unified interface, the HUD demonstrates how critical mission information could be presented to astronauts without requiring them to navigate multiple separate monitoring systems.
3. Integration Between Both Systems
A defining feature of Martian Guide is the direct integration between its two modules. The Astronaut HUD includes a dedicated 3D Map button, which opens the 3D Mars Explorer. This connection demonstrates how an astronaut-oriented interface could provide access to a broader planetary visualization environment when geographical awareness or mission navigation is required.
Rather than functioning as two independent demonstrations, both modules form a unified concept: the HUD represents the astronaut's immediate operational interface, while the 3D Explorer provides a wider understanding of the surrounding planetary environment.
4. Benefits and Intended Impact
Martian Guide explores several potential benefits for future human exploration missions:
Improved Situational Awareness: Bringing geographical information and essential astronaut telemetry into an accessible digital environment.
Mission Preparation: Helping students, researchers, and future mission planners visualize Mars, its exploration history, and important geographical features.
Human-Machine Interaction: Demonstrating an approach to organizing critical information within an astronaut-oriented interface.
Educational Accessibility: Making planetary exploration more interactive and understandable for students and the wider public.
Future Expandability: Establishing a foundation for incorporating more advanced navigation systems, real mission datasets, and additional astronaut-support capabilities.
5. Project Vision
Our objective is to go beyond creating a visualization of Mars. We want to demonstrate how an interconnected digital environment could help bridge the gap between planetary exploration data and the information astronauts need during a mission.
